## A Working Learning Product

**Career Intelligence Lab** is my own independently developed learning product for evidence-based career exploration, career preparedness, and workforce readiness.

It was designed to be delivered through **workshops and digital learning**, with a framework that can be adapted for different learner groups and contexts.

The project brings together:

**self-awareness + occupational information + labour-market evidence + interactive learning + reflection**

The underlying purpose is to help learners move from general career ideas toward a more realistic understanding of themselves, the world of work, and their possible next steps.

---

## 🎯 The Design Problem

I did not want to create a resource that simply tells learners about occupations.

The more useful question was:

> **How can a learning experience help someone make a better career decision?**

That requires more than information.

A learner may know what an occupation is called but still not understand:

* whether it fits their interests or abilities
* what skills the work actually requires
* how those skills relate to their current capabilities
* what the labour market is signalling
* where they may need to develop further
* what practical next step makes sense

So I designed the experience around **career preparedness**, not information delivery.

---

## 🧭 From Information to Preparedness

The learning journey is designed to move through a sequence:

```text
Self-Awareness
      ↓
Career Exploration
      ↓
Occupational Evidence
      ↓
Labour-Market Awareness
      ↓
Reflection
      ↓
Career Direction
      ↓
Practical Next Steps
```

The learner is not simply exposed to information.

The learner is asked to **explore, compare, interpret, reflect, and make decisions**.

That distinction is important to my broader training approach.

---

## 🔗 What the Learner Is Developing

Career Intelligence Lab helps participants develop several connected capabilities.

### 🔎 Informed Decision-Making

Use occupational and labour-market evidence to make career decisions based on more than assumptions.

### 🧩 Skill Awareness

Understand the technical knowledge, transferable skills, and capabilities associated with different occupations.

### 🎯 Self-to-Career Alignment

Connect personal interests, abilities, and self-awareness with realistic career possibilities.

### 📊 Labour-Market Awareness

Understand where opportunities are emerging and what available evidence suggests about changing work.

### 🧭 Career Direction

Translate exploration and reflection into practical next steps.

The aim is not to tell someone which career to choose.

It is to help the learner make a **better-informed decision for himself or herself**.

---

## 🧠 Learning Rather Than Information Delivery

The instructional design deliberately combines information with activities that require the learner to do something with it.

For example:

**Information**

→ explore an occupation

**Analysis**

→ examine the skills and requirements

**Self-reflection**

→ compare those requirements with personal interests and capabilities

**Interpretation**

→ identify areas of alignment and development

**Planning**

→ determine practical next steps

This changes the role of the content.

The content becomes a resource for **thinking and decision-making**, rather than the final learning outcome.

## 💻 Digital Learning Implementation

The Career Intelligence Lab has been implemented as a digital learning experience using **MoodleCloud**.

The repository documents:

* the learning framework
* the participant journey
* career intelligence reports
* reflection and planning activities
* the Moodle learning environment
* the instructional-design principles behind the experience

This provides a concrete example of how I connect:

**learning design → digital environment → learner activity → reflection → practical application**

---

## 📊 Evidence Is Used as Part of the Learning

The Lab uses occupational and labour-market information as material for exploration and interpretation.

The learner is encouraged to ask:

> What does the evidence tell me?

and then:

> What does that mean for me?

This is an important part of the design.

The objective is not to overwhelm the learner with labour-market data.

It is to help the learner **make sense of evidence and connect it to a personal decision**.

## 🔄 How This Reflects My Training Systems Approach

The project demonstrates several principles from this portfolio:

| Training Systems Principle           | Career Intelligence Lab                          |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Start with the desired outcome       | Career preparedness and informed decision-making |
| Design around what people need to do | Explore, analyse, reflect, decide, plan          |
| Connect information to action        | Occupational evidence → career decisions         |
| Use appropriate technology           | MoodleCloud supports the learning journey        |
| Make learning active                 | Interactive exploration and reflection           |
| Use evidence                         | Occupational and labour-market information       |
| Support application                  | Personal career planning and next steps          |
| Improve over time                    | The learning product has continued to evolve     |

The point is not that every training project should look like Career Intelligence Lab.

The point is that the **same design logic can be applied to different learning problems**.

## 🌱 Why This Matters for Workforce Readiness

I am particularly interested in the potential of this approach for **young people entering the workforce**.

The challenge is not simply helping people understand what jobs exist.

It is helping them develop a more realistic understanding of:

**their capabilities**

**the skills work requires**

**how work is changing**

**where they need to develop**

**how to make practical career decisions**

This becomes increasingly relevant as organizations adopt new digital and AI-enabled ways of working.

Career preparedness therefore needs to connect **self-awareness with the realities of work**.
