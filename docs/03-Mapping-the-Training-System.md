# 🗺️ Mapping the Training System

> **Before I design or change training, I want to understand how the need moves through the system—from the initial request to learning, application, support, and improvement.**

---

## Why I Map the System

A training activity rarely begins when a learner opens a course.

There is usually a sequence of events before that happens.

Someone identifies a need.

Information is gathered.

A learning activity is planned.

People are scheduled or enrolled.

Materials are prepared.

The learning takes place.

The learner returns to the workplace.

Questions or support needs may appear.

Information is recorded.

Evidence becomes available.

That evidence can then inform the next improvement.

I use mapping to make those connections visible.

---

## 🔄 The Training Flow

A simple representation of the system is:

```text
Need
  │
  ▼
Plan
  │
  ▼
Prepare
  │
  ▼
Learn
  │
  ▼
Apply
  │
  ▼
Support
  │
  ▼
Measure
  │
  ▼
Improve
  │
  └──────────────► back to the next need
```
The exact steps will vary by organization.

The purpose of the map is not to impose one process.

It is to understand how the process actually works.


---
## 🔎 What I Look At at Each Stage
**1. Need**

I first try to understand why the training or learning support is being requested.

Questions include:

- What has changed?
- What problem is being experienced?
- Who is affected?
- What needs to happen differently?
- Is training actually part of the solution?

A clear understanding of the need prevents the training activity from becoming an end in itself.


---
**2. Plan**

Once the need is understood, the learning activity has to be organized.

This may involve:

identifying learners
determining timing
coordinating people
identifying resources
confirming requirements
defining responsibilities
planning the learning experience

At this stage, I look for dependencies.

For example, does the person responsible for creating the content have the information needed to do so?

Does scheduling depend on information being available?

Does the learner need access to a system before the training can be effective?


---
**3. Prepare**

Preparation connects the intended learning experience with what the learner will actually encounter.

This can include:

- learning materials
- demonstrations
- activities
- presentations
- digital resources
- LMS setup
- instructions
- job aids
- reference materials

I also consider whether the materials reflect the current process and technology.

A well-designed resource can still create problems if the underlying information is outdated.


---
**4. Learn**

This is the visible part of training, but it is only one part of the system.

I consider:

- what the learner is expected to understand
- what the learner needs to practice
- how the learning is delivered
- what opportunities exist for interaction
- where learners may encounter difficulty
- how understanding or performance is checked

My instructional-design background leads me to think about learning as an active process rather than simply delivering information.


---
**5. Apply**

The real test of learning begins when the learner returns to the work.

I ask:

>Can the learner use what was learned in the situation where it is needed?

This is where gaps can become visible.

The learner may understand the concept but struggle with the actual workflow.

The learner may know the process but have difficulty using the technology.

The learner may remember the training but need a reference resource at the point of work.

These observations are valuable because they connect learning with performance.


---
**6. Support**

Support provides another view of the system.

Questions, requests for help, repeated errors, and requests for clarification can reveal where learners are encountering friction.

I look for patterns rather than treating every question as an isolated event.

For example:

```
Individual Question
        │
        ▼
Repeated Questions
        │
        ▼
Identify Pattern
        │
        ▼
Investigate Cause
        │
        ├──────────────► Training Issue
        │
        ├──────────────► Documentation Issue
        │
        ├──────────────► Process Issue
        │
        └──────────────► Technology Issue
```
This helps turn support activity into useful information about the learning environment.


---
**7. Measure**

Measurement helps determine what happened after the learning activity.

Depending on the situation, I may look at:

- participation
- completion
- learner feedback
- assessment results
- LMS activity
- recurring support questions
- performance information
- content issues
- time taken to resolve recurring problems

My background in economics and learning analytics helps me approach data as evidence for decision-making.

I do not assume that one metric tells the whole story.

I look for patterns and relationships.


---
**8. Improve**

The final stage connects the system back to the beginning.

Evidence can lead to:

- updated learning content
- improved instructions
- a new job aid
- additional practice
- clearer communication
- a process adjustment
- better learner support
- a technology-related intervention

The improvement then becomes part of the next cycle.
        

```
Measure
   │
   ▼
Interpret
   │
   ▼
Decide
   │
   ▼
Improve
   │
   ▼
Observe Again
   │
   └──────────────► Continuous Improvement
```


## 🧩 Mapping Dependencies
One reason I use system maps is to make dependencies easier to see.


For example:

```
Accurate Information
        │
        ▼
Accurate Learning Content
        │
        ▼
Prepared Learner
        │
        ▼
Successful Application
        │
        ▼
Useful Performance Evidence
```

If the information at the beginning is wrong or outdated, the problem can travel through the entire system.

That means improving the final learning activity alone may not solve the underlying issue.

The map helps identify where the dependency begins.


---
## 👥 Ownership Matters

A training system involves different people and responsibilities.

I therefore consider:

- Who owns the process?
- Who creates the learning?
- Who validates the information?
- Who delivers or facilitates the training?
- Who maintains the LMS or learning environment?
- Who supports the learner?
- Who uses the resulting information?
- Who is responsible for approving or updating the content?

Clear ownership reduces the risk of information becoming outdated or tasks falling between responsibilities.

It also makes improvement easier because there is a clearer connection between an observation and the person who can act on it.


---
## 📍 Finding the Friction Points

When I map a system, I look for places where work becomes unnecessarily difficult.

Typical signals include:

- repeated questions
- repeated manual work
- unclear handoffs
- duplicate information
- outdated materials
- missing instructions
- inconsistent communication
- learners unable to find what they need
- technology that does not fit the workflow
- information that is difficult to maintain

These are not automatically problems.

They are signals worth investigating.

The purpose of the map is to ask:

> What is creating the friction, and what is the smallest useful change that could improve it?


---
## 🧭 A Practical Mapping Method

When I need to understand a training environment, I can start with a simple four-step process:

**1. Trace the Flow**

Follow the journey from the initial need through learning and application.

**2. Identify the Connections**

Note where people, processes, information, technology, and learning depend on one another.

**3. Find the Friction**

Look for delays, confusion, repeated questions, missing information, or unnecessary work.

**4. Decide Where to Investigate**

Prioritize the connections that appear most important to the learner, the work, or the organization.

This keeps the analysis practical.

I am not trying to map everything.

I am trying to understand enough of the system to make a better decision.


---
📐 The Map Is a Working Tool

A system map does not need to be complicated.

It can be a simple flow, a process diagram, a table, or a conversation with the people involved.

Its value comes from making relationships visible.

I use the map to help answer questions such as:

> Where does the need begin?

> What information moves through the system?

> Who depends on whom?

> Where does the learner encounter friction?

> What happens after training?

> What evidence comes back into the system?

> Where could a small intervention create a meaningful improvement?


---
## 💡 The Principle Behind the Map

My goal is not to create a perfect diagram of an organization.

My goal is to develop a useful picture of how the learning system works.

That picture helps me move from:

**Training request**

to

**Understanding**

to

**Better intervention**

to

**Evidence**

to

**Improvement**

The map is therefore not the final product.

It is a tool for making better training decisions.
