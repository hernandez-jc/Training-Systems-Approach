# 🧭 My Training Approach

> **I don't treat training as a collection of courses. I look at how people, processes, information, technology, and learning interact—and use that understanding to design training that works in the real world.**

---

## 🎯 Where I Start

When I approach a training need, I don't begin with a platform, a course template, or a particular type of content.

**I start with the work.**

I want to understand:

>- What does the person need to do?
>- What does successful performance look like?
>- What information do they need?
>- Which people, processes, and technologies are involved?
>- Where is the task likely to become difficult?
>- What could prevent the learner from applying the training?
>- What support will be needed afterward?

This helps me distinguish between a **learning problem** and a problem that may actually sit somewhere else in the system.

Sometimes the answer is a course.

Sometimes it is a demonstration, screen recording, job aid, quick-reference guide, or clearer documentation.

And sometimes the training is not the problem at all.

---

## 🔗 I Look at the Connections

My approach is grounded in systems thinking.

I look at five interconnected elements:

| Element | What I look for |
|---|---|
| 👥 **People** | Who learns, performs, supports, validates, or maintains the work? |
| ⚙️ **Processes** | What needs to happen, in what sequence, and where can friction occur? |
| 📚 **Information** | What knowledge, instructions, records, and decisions need to move through the system? |
| 💻 **Technology** | Which tools support the work, and where can technology create or remove friction? |
| 🧠 **Learning** | What does the learner need to understand, practice, remember, or perform? |

The categories are useful, but the **relationships between them** are what I pay closest attention to.

A change in one element can affect the others.

For example:

> A software change can affect a process.  
> The process change can make existing instructions inaccurate.  
> The inaccurate instructions can create learner questions.  
> Repeated questions can reveal a gap in the training or documentation.  
> That evidence can then guide the next improvement.

This is why I try to understand the **system around the learning**, not only the learning material itself.

---

## 🛠️ Design Around the Task

I believe learning content should have a clear reason for existing.

Before creating a video, lesson, guide, or other learning resource, I ask:

> **What does this resource help the learner do?**

That question helps determine the appropriate format.

### A practical decision

**Need to understand something?**  
→ Provide a clear explanation.

**Need to see how something is done?**  
→ Demonstrate it.

**Need to perform a task repeatedly?**  
→ Provide a practical job aid or quick-reference resource.

**Need structured learning and practice?**  
→ Build an appropriate learning experience.

**Need support while doing the work?**  
→ Make the relevant information easy to find and use.

The objective is not to create more content.

**The objective is to create the right support for the task.**

- clear

## 🔄 Learning Does Not End When the Course Ends

One of the most important parts of my approach is what happens **after** training.

Questions, support requests, learner feedback, completion information, and performance evidence can all provide useful signals.

A recurring question may point to:

- a difficult process
- unclear instructions
- an incomplete learning resource
- information that is difficult to find
- a change in technology
- a mismatch between the training and the actual task

I therefore see learner support as more than a reactive service.

### Support can become evidence.

And evidence can become improvement.

```text
Learner / Employee
        │
        ▼
     Question
        │
        ▼
   Identify Pattern
        │
        ▼
 Understand the Cause
        │
        ▼
 Improve Learning / Support / Process
        │
        ▼
      Observe
        │
        └──────────────► Improve Again
```
## 📈 My Improvement Mindset

When I encounter a problem, I try to solve two things:

1. The immediate problem

Help the person move forward.

2. The underlying problem

Understand why the issue occurred and whether something in the system should change.

My general cycle is:

Observe → Understand → Design → Apply → Measure → Improve

This does not mean making every training activity complicated.

**It means being deliberate about where a problem comes from, choosing an appropriate response, and using evidence to decide what should happen next.**

## 💡 My Guiding Question

I don't begin with:

>**"Which tool should I use?"**

I begin with:

>**"What is happening, what needs to happen, and what is getting in the way?"**

From there, I can determine whether the right response is:

**learning content · digital support · documentation · learner support · process improvement · or a combination of these.**

That is the foundation of my **Training Systems Approach.**
