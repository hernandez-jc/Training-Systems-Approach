# 🎯 Designing Learning Around Work

> **I design learning around what people need to do—not simply around what information needs to be delivered.**

---

## Start With the Work

A learning experience should have a clear relationship to the work it supports.

Before deciding what to teach, I want to understand what the learner will actually need to do.

I ask:

- What task will the learner perform?
- What does successful performance look like?
- What decisions will the learner need to make?
- What information will the learner need?
- What could make the task difficult?
- What opportunities will the learner have to practice?
- What support will be available when the learner returns to the work?

These questions help me move from **content-first design** to **performance-focused design**.

---

## 🧠 Not Everything Needs to Be Taught

One of the principles I bring to instructional design is that learners do not always need more explanation.

Sometimes they need:

- an opportunity to discover a concept
- an example
- a demonstration
- guided practice
- feedback
- a reference resource
- a chance to apply the skill
- information available at the point of need

This is especially important when people already have relevant experience.

Rather than explaining everything in advance, I can structure activities that allow learners to **observe, explore, make connections, and reach conclusions.**

This is consistent with my use of an ***inductive teaching approach.***


---
## 🔍 Learning Through Discovery

In an inductive approach, I do not automatically begin by giving the learner the answer. ( When applicable , sometimes the rule needs to be upfront :wink:)

I can begin with:

an example → an observation → a question → exploration → a pattern → a conclusion

For example:

Example
   │
   ▼
What do you notice?
   │
   ▼
Explore
   │
   ▼
Compare
   │
   ▼
Identify the pattern
   │
   ▼
Form the concept
   │
   ▼
Apply it

The facilitator's role is important.

- I guide the process without taking away the learner's opportunity to think.
- The objective is not to make learning difficult.
- It is to make the learner an active participant in understanding.

## 🎥 Choosing the Right Learning Format

I do not assume that every learning need should become a traditional course.

The task helps determine the format.

| Learning or performance need   | Possible response                        |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Understand a concept           | Explanation, example, or guided activity |
| See how something is performed | Demonstration or screen recording        |
| Practice a procedure           | Guided practice                          |
| Remember a sequence            | Job aid or quick-reference guide         |
| Make a decision                | Scenario or case-based activity          |
| Apply knowledge to work        | Authentic task or practice activity      |
| Find information during work   | Point-of-need resource                   |
| Build a broader capability     | Structured learning experience           |

**The format is a design decision.**

>It should serve the task rather than become the objective itself.

## 🎬 Digital Learning Content

I have practical experience creating digital learning content and instructional demonstrations.

My experience includes:

- Camtasia for screen capture and instructional demonstrations ( Interactive Video SCORM Compliant Questions as needed ) 
- MoodleCloud for digital learning delivery
- Digital learning activities and resources ( e.g. authoring tools, Storyline Articulate + Javascript, iSpring)
- instructional design for structured learning experiences
- multimedia principles for presenting information clearly
- LMS and learning analytics to examine learner activity

**I am interested in digital learning because it can make learning more accessible and flexible.**

But digital delivery alone does not make learning effective.

**The important question remains:**

> **Does the digital experience help the learner understand, practice, and apply what is needed?**


---
## 🎨 Multimedia Should Support Learning

When I create digital learning content, I consider how information is presented rather than simply putting more information on screen.

I aim to:

- reduce unnecessary information
- make the purpose of each element clear
- guide attention
- use visuals when they genuinely support understanding
- separate complex information into manageable parts
- connect explanation with demonstration
- give learners opportunities to process and apply information

This reflects my use of **instructional design and scientifically informed multimedia learning principles.**

The technology is a means of communication.

The learning objective remains the priority.

## 🧑‍🏫 Facilitation Is Part of the Design

Good learning design does not end when the material is created.

Facilitation affects what learners notice, discuss, question, and apply.

My facilitation approach emphasizes:

- creating space for learners to think
- asking useful questions
- guiding discovery
- connecting activities to real work
- encouraging participation
- responding to learner needs
- avoiding unnecessary explanation when learners can discover the answer themselves

This is particularly useful when learners bring existing professional knowledge into the room.

The facilitator does not need to be the only source of knowledge.

The learning environment can help participants learn from the task, the activity, the information, and one another.

## 🌎 Designing for Real People

Learning design also has to account for the people using it.

My experience working in both English and Spanish has strengthened my attention to clarity, terminology, context, and audience.

When information is technical, specialized, or used in a regulated environment, small communication problems can have larger consequences.

I therefore pay attention to:

- terminology
- clarity
- sequence
- context
- examples
- instructions
- language
- accessibility of information
- opportunities to check understanding

The goal is not to make complex information simplistic.

It is to make it understandable and usable.

### 📊 Evidence Improves the Design

I use evidence to ask whether the learning experience is doing what it was intended to do.

Useful signals may include:

- learner feedback
- assessment performance
- participation
- LMS activity
- questions raised during learning
- recurring support requests
- observations from facilitators
- difficulty applying the learning
- changes in workplace performance

My background in Economics, data analysis, learning analytics, and LMS analytics helps me approach these signals analytically.

I do not expect data to explain everything.

I use it alongside observation, learner feedback, and understanding of the work.

        │

        │
        ▼

        │

        ▼

        │

        │

The cycle is deliberately practical.

I do not want to design learning in isolation from the environment where it will be used.


## 💡 My Design Principle

I try to keep one question in view throughout the design process:

> **What will this learner be able to do differently because of this learning experience?**

That question helps keep the design connected to performance.

It also helps prevent a common problem:

**creating learning content simply because content is expected.**

Instead, I want every element of the learning experience to have a purpose.
