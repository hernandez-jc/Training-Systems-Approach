# 🛠️ How I Apply the Approach

> **I use the Training Systems Approach to move from a training request to a practical understanding of the work, the learning need, and the most useful intervention.**

---

## Start With the Request

A training request is the starting point, not necessarily the diagnosis.

I first want to understand:

- What has prompted the request?
- Who is affected?
- What needs to happen differently?
- What is happening today?
- What would better performance look like?

This gives me enough context to investigate before deciding what to build.

---

## 1. Understand the Work

I look at the task the person needs to perform.

```text
What needs to happen?
        │
        ▼
Who performs the task?
        │
        ▼
What information is needed?
        │
        ▼
What tools and processes are involved?
        │
        ▼
Where can the task become difficult?

The objective is to understand the work from the learner's perspective.

## 2. Identify the Learning Need

Once the task is clearer, I distinguish between what the person needs to do and what the person needs to learn.

I ask:

- What knowledge is required?
- What skill needs to be practiced?
- What decisions need to be made?
- What information needs to be available?
- What can be learned through practice?
- What may need support rather than training?

This prevents the learning solution from becoming larger than the actual need.

## 3. Examine the Surrounding System

I then consider the relationships between:

**People · Processes · Information · Technology · Learning**

I look for dependencies and points of friction.

For example:

>Is the learner struggling because the concept is difficult, because the process is unclear, because the information is outdated, or because the technology does not support the task well?

The answer affects the intervention.


---
## 4. Choose the Appropriate Response

I then decide what kind of support is most useful.

```text
Identified Need
      │
      ├── Learning Need
      │       └── Learning Experience
      │
      ├── Practice Need
      │       └── Guided Practice
      │
      ├── Information Need
      │       └── Job Aid / Reference
      │
      ├── Support Need
      │       └── Point-of-Need Guidance
      │
      └── Process / Technology Issue
              └── Investigate the Underlying Cause
```
The response may be one intervention or a combination.

The important point is that the response follows the analysis.

## 5. Design and Facilitate

When learning is the appropriate response, I design around the task.

Depending on the situation, this may include:

- examples
- demonstrations
- guided activities
- practice
- discussion
- digital learning content
- screen recordings
- job aids
- opportunities for application

My facilitation approach encourages learners to think, explore, make connections, and apply what they are learning.

I do not believe that more explanation automatically produces better learning.


---
## 6. Support Application
The work continues after the learning experience.

I want the learner to be able to apply the learning in the real environment.

I therefore consider:

- what support is available
- where information can be found
- whether reference materials are current
- what questions emerge
- where learners experience difficulty

These observations provide useful evidence about whether the learning is working as intended.


---
## 7. Learn From the Evidence

I use a small set of practical questions:

- Can people access what they need?
- Are they participating?
- Can they apply the learning?
- Where are they struggling?
- What questions keep recurring?
- What changed after an intervention?

I then use the answers to decide what needs attention.

```text
Evidence
   │
   ▼
Interpretation
   │
   ▼
Action
   │
   ▼
Measurement
   │
   └──────────────► Improve
```
The objective is not to create a large measurement system.

It is to make better decisions.


---
## 🧩 A Complete Example

Imagine a team is asking for training because employees are having difficulty completing a new digital process.

I would not immediately begin by creating a course.

I would first investigate.

**The request**

Employees need training on the new process."

The investigation

I would ask:

What changed?
What does the new process require?
Which steps are causing difficulty?
What information do employees need?
Which technology is involved?
What training already exists?
What questions are employees asking?
The finding

Suppose the issue is not a lack of general knowledge.

Instead, employees understand the purpose of the process but struggle with one specific sequence of steps in the new system.

The response

A full course may not be necessary.

A better response could be:

short demonstration + guided practice + quick-reference job aid

The feedback

After implementation, recurring questions and learner feedback can show whether the intervention helped.

If the same difficulty continues, I investigate again.

The solution can then be adjusted.

🔄 The Approach in Practice

The complete cycle is:

```text
TRAINING REQUEST
       │
       ▼
UNDERSTAND THE WORK
       │
       ▼
IDENTIFY THE NEED
       │
       ▼
EXAMINE THE SYSTEM
       │
       ▼
CHOOSE THE RESPONSE
       │
       ▼
DESIGN / FACILITATE
       │
       ▼
APPLY
       │
       ▼
SUPPORT
       │
       ▼
OBSERVE
       │
       ▼
IMPROVE
       │
       └──────────────► back to the work
```

This is the practical expression of my Training Systems Approach.

## 🎯 The Principle

I try to keep the approach simple:

>** Understand the work. Identify what is getting in the way. Decide whether learning is the right intervention. Design around the task. Observe what happens. Improve.**

That is how I approach training as a system rather than as a collection of courses.
